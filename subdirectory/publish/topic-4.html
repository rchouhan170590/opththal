Detailed Anatomy of the Sclera

Scleral Thickness Variation

🟢 Basic

The Sclera: "The White of the Eye"

The sclera makes up the posterior 5/6 of the outer fibrous tunic. It is opaque, tough, and gives the eyeball its structural rigidity. Unlike the cornea, the scleral collagen fibers are arranged irregularly rather than in a lattice, which is exactly why the sclera is opaque while the cornea is clear.

During strabismus surgery, the sclera is thinnest (0.3mm) just posterior to muscle insertions. Inadvertent perforation here can cause retinal tear or vitreous hemorrhage.

🔴 Advanced

Lamina Cribrosa: The Glaucoma Site

The lamina cribrosa is the sieve-like portion of the sclera through which the optic nerve fibers exit the eye. It has multiple small openings (foramina) through which nerve fiber bundles pass.

In glaucoma: Raised intraocular pressure pushes the lamina cribrosa backward. As it bows posteriorly, it pinches the nerve fiber bundles passing through its foramina. That mechanical compression leads to optic nerve damage and progressive visual field loss.
  • Normal IOP: 10–21 mmHg
  • In glaucoma: optic cup enlarges (C/D ratio >0.6)
  • The lamina cribrosa is the primary site of mechanical injury
🟡 Intermediate

Episclera & Tenon's Capsule

Several layers cover the sclera externally:

  • Episclera: Thin, vascularized loose connective tissue directly on the sclera. Provides nutrition to the avascular sclera. Contains the deep episcleral venous plexus (receives aqueous drainage).
  • Tenon's Capsule (Fascia Bulbi): Fibrous membrane that envelops the eyeball. Acts as a socket for eye movement.
  • Conjunctiva: Outermost, thin mucous membrane.
💎

Episcleritis vs Scleritis: Episcleritis is a superficial, moveable, pink injection that settles on its own. Scleritis goes deeper, with a characteristic bluish-red hue, severe pain, tenderness, and a strong association with autoimmune conditions like RA or lupus.
