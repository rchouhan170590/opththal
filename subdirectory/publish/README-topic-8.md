## One-line pitch

The largest intraocular compartment is mostly water held in a collagen–hyaluronan gel — attached strongest at the vitreous base, prone to liquefaction with age, and central to PVD and retinal traction stories.

## Suggested structure

### 1. Hook

- Vitreous fills lens–retina space; **~4 mL** — largest compartment.

### 2. What is the vitreous?

- Clear **gel**; structural role for globe shape.

### 3. Composition

- Collagen fibrils + **hyaluronic acid**; aging → **synchysis** (liquefaction).

### 4. Vitreous base

- Straddles **ora serrata**; **strongest** vitreoretinal adhesion — explain implications for traction.

### 5. Synchysis → PVD

- Posterior cortex peels from retina; common age-related event.
- Symptoms narrative: floaters; **educational** note to seek care for sudden curtain/field loss (non-alarmist, encourage exam).

### 6. Floaters & retinal tears

- Weiss ring; traction at horseshoe tears — keep anatomy-first, avoid diagnosing readers.

### 7. Close

- “Understanding PVD is understanding where the gel sticks — and where it lets go.”
