Lens Structure — Cross Section

🟢 Basic

The Lens — Fine Focus

The lens is a transparent, biconvex, elastic structure located behind the iris and pupil. Unlike the cornea (fixed power), the lens can change its shape to adjust focus from far to near — this is called accommodation.

🟡 Intermediate

Lens Structure

  • Capsule: Thickest basement membrane in the body. Thicker anteriorly. Contains the epithelium inside.
  • Epithelium: Single layer of cuboidal cells beneath anterior capsule only. Divides to form new lens fibers.
  • Cortex: New, soft lens fibers (formed from differentiating epithelial cells).
  • Nucleus: Hard, compressed, older fibers in the center. Multiple zones: embryonic, fetal, infantile, adult.
  • Zonules of Zinn: Fibrillin-containing suspensory ligaments attaching lens to ciliary body.
🔴 Advanced

Mechanism of Accommodation

Near vision: Ciliary muscle contracts → ring of ciliary body becomes smaller → zonules relax → lens becomes more spherical/convex → focal power increases → near objects focused.

Helmholtz Theory: Accommodation is driven by changes in lens shape due to zonule tension, not lens movement.

Far vision: Ciliary muscle relaxes → zonules become taut → lens flattened → focal power decreases → distant objects focused.

🔴 Advanced

Lens Growth Throughout Life → Presbyopia → Cataract

The lens never stops growing. New fibers are added at the periphery throughout life, and the older fibers get pushed toward the center and compressed. Over time this builds up a progressively harder nucleus.

💎

Cataract surgery (phacoemulsification): An ultrasound probe breaks up the hard nucleus, the cortex is aspirated, and an artificial IOL is placed inside the capsular bag. The posterior capsule stays in to hold the IOL in position.
