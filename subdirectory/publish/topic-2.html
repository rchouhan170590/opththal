Anatomy of the Eyeball (Tunics)

Cross-section of the Human Eyeball

🟢 Basic

The Three Tunics

Think of the eyeball as having three concentric coats wrapped around each other, like layers of an onion. Each coat has its own job:

1
Outer Fibrous Tunic
Cornea (anterior 1/6) + Sclera (posterior 5/6) — Protection & shape
2
Middle Vascular Tunic (Uvea)
Iris + Ciliary Body + Choroid — Nutrition, blood supply & regulation
3
Inner Neural Tunic
Retina — Light detection & visual transduction
🟡 Intermediate

Outer Fibrous Tunic

  • Cornea (anterior 1/6): Clear, watch-glass shaped, major refracting medium (~43D of power)
  • Sclera (posterior 5/6): "White of the eye," tough and opaque, provides structural support
The cornea and sclera together make up the outer coat. Where they meet is the Limbus, one of the most clinically important zones in the eye.
🟡 Intermediate

Middle Vascular Tunic (Uvea)

  • Iris: Pigmented diaphragm, controls pupil size (light regulation)
  • Ciliary Body: Aqueous humor production + accommodation (lens focusing)
  • Choroid: Dense vascular layer supplying the outer 2/3 of the retina
🔴 Advanced

Axial Length & Refractive Error

The eye has a normal axial length of about 24 mm. This single measurement drives almost everything in clinical refractive work.

24mm
Normal Axial Length
-3D
Per 1mm increase (myopia)
+3D
Per 1mm decrease (hyperopia)
💎

Before cataract surgery, biometry measures axial length precisely to choose the right IOL power. A 0.1 mm error in that measurement translates to about 0.3D of refractive surprise in the postoperative result.

🔴 Advanced

The Limbus: A Critical Zone

The limbus is the junction between the cornea, sclera, and conjunctiva, running about 1 mm wide all the way around.

Contents of the Limbus:
  • Corneal epithelial stem cells (in Palisades of Vogt)
  • Trabecular meshwork (aqueous drainage)
  • Canal of Schlemm
  • Surgical landmark for incisions
⚠️

Limbal Stem Cell Deficiency (LSCD): When these stem cells are destroyed (in chemical burns or Stevens-Johnson syndrome), conjunctival tissue grows over the cornea. The cornea vascularizes, becomes opaque, and vision is lost.
