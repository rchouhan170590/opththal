## Suggested article structure

### 1. Hook (2–4 short paragraphs)

- The orbit is not “just a socket”: it’s a cone-shaped space for the globe, muscles, vessels, lacrimal apparatus, and nerves.
- Tease why wall thickness varies and why that drives trauma and infection patterns.

### 2. What is the orbit?

- Pear-shaped bony cavity; apex posterior, base anterior.
- ~40 mL volume (fact from your page).
- Emphasize: each wall differs in thickness and clinical risk.

### 3. The seven bones and four walls

- **Roof:** frontal + lesser wing of sphenoid (lacrimal gland fossa).
- **Medial:** ethmoid (lamina papyracea), lacrimal, maxillary, sphenoid — **thinnest wall**.
- **Floor:** maxillary, zygomatic, palatine — **blow-out fractures**.
- **Lateral:** zygomatic + greater wing of sphenoid — **strongest wall**.

### 4. Orbital apex: optic canal, SOF, IOF

- **Optic canal:** CN II, ophthalmic artery.
- **SOF:** CN III, IV, VI; V1; superior ophthalmic vein.
- **IOF:** V2; infraorbital vessels.
- **Pearl to highlight:** orbital apex syndrome — ophthalmoplegia plus optic nerve involvement → lesion at apex affecting canal + fissure.

### 5. Annulus of Zinn (common tendinous ring)

- Four recti originate here; defines **retrobulbar** vs **peribulbar** space.
- Brief contrast: retrobulbar block (inside cone) vs peribulbar (outside) — onset, volume, safety tradeoffs.

### 6. Clinical pearls (high engagement on Medium)

- **Lamina papyracea / ethmoid sinusitis → orbital cellulitis** (especially pediatric proptosis context).
- **Blow-out fracture:** floor, inferior rectus entrapment, diplopia on upgaze.

### 7. Close

- One sentence: “Anatomy isn’t trivia — it’s the language of orbital emergencies.”
- Optional: point readers to Topic 2 (eyeball) in your series.
