🟢 Basic

The Retina: The Eye's Camera Film

The retina is the eye's light-sensitive inner lining. Embryologically it grows out of the brain (neuroectoderm), and it essentially behaves like brain tissue. It converts incoming light into electrical signals that travel along the optic nerve to the visual cortex.

🟡 Intermediate

Macula and Fovea

Macula lutea: ~5.5mm diameter area temporal to the optic disc. Responsible for central, detailed vision (reading, recognizing faces).

  • Fovea: Central depression (~1.5mm) within the macula
  • Foveola: The very center (~0.35mm), only cones, no rods
  • Umbo: The very bottom of the foveola — light reflex seen on fundoscopy
Why the fovea is so sharp: The inner retinal layers are pushed aside laterally, so light lands directly on the photoreceptors without passing through anything. There are no blood vessels here either (the foveal avascular zone, FAZ). Cones are packed at their highest density at this single spot.
💎

Macular degeneration (ARMD) hits this zone specifically, causing a central scotoma. The peripheral vision usually holds on, which is why patients say they cannot read a face but can still walk around the room.

🔴 Advanced

Outer Blood-Retinal Barrier (RPE)

The Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE) is the outermost layer of the retina. It forms the outer blood-retinal barrier through tight junctions (zonulae occludentes) between RPE cells.

Functions of RPE:
  • Blood-retinal barrier (outer)
  • Phagocytosis of shed photoreceptor outer segments
  • Vitamin A (retinol) metabolism / Visual cycle
  • Ion transport to maintain subretinal space
  • Absorbs stray light (melanin)
⚠️

Retinal Detachment: Fluid gets between the RPE and photoreceptors (between layers 1 and 2). Once separated, photoreceptors are cut off from their choroidal blood supply and begin to die. Prolonged detachment means permanent vision loss, which is why it is a surgical emergency.

🔴 Advanced

Retinal Detachment — Mechanism

The photoreceptors (sensory retina) are attached to the RPE only by a potential space — the subretinal space. This space can fill with fluid in retinal detachment:
