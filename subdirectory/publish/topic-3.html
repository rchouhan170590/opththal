The 6 Layers of the Cornea (Cross-section)

🟢 Basic

What is the Cornea?

The cornea is the clear, watch-glass-shaped front of the eye and its major refracting surface, contributing about 43 Diopters out of the eye's total ~60D. For it to do this job, it has to stay perfectly transparent, which depends on the precise architecture of all its layers.

🟡 Intermediate

The 6 Layers: Anterior to Posterior

1
Epithelium (~50μm)
5 cell layers (1 columnar basal, 2-3 wing cells, 2 squamous). Rapidly regenerates. Regenerates from limbal stem cells.
2
Bowman's Layer (~15μm)
Acellular condensed collagen. Any injury here heals as a permanent scar, not normal tissue. No regeneration.
3
Stroma (~500μm)
90% of corneal thickness. Regular lattice of collagen fibrils (Type I) with equal spacing = TRANSPARENCY (Maurice's Theory). Contains keratocytes.
4
Dua's Layer (~15μm)
Discovered in 2013. Between stroma and Descemet's. Strong, acellular. Clinically important in "big bubble" DALK surgery.
5
Descemet's Membrane (~10μm)
Basement membrane of endothelium. Thickens with age. Rupture = Haab's striae (in congenital glaucoma). Very elastic.
6
Endothelium (single layer)
Single layer of hexagonal cells acting as a pump: keeps the stroma dehydrated and therefore clear. Does not regenerate.
🔴 Advanced

Stromal Transparency: Maurice's Theory

The stroma is 90% of the corneal thickness, yet it is perfectly transparent. The reason lies in the organization of its collagen fibrils: uniform diameter (~25nm), uniform spacing, and arranged in a precise lattice.

Maurice's Theory: This regular arrangement causes destructive interference of scattered light waves, so all the scattered light cancels out and only the forward-directed light passes through. Disrupt the spacing (as in corneal oedema) and you get opacity.
🔴 Advanced

The Endothelial Pump

The endothelium keeps the cornea clear by actively pumping water out of the stroma. A dehydrated stroma is a transparent stroma. Stop the pump and the stroma swells.

  • Normal cell density: ~2500 to 3000 cells/mm²
  • Critical threshold: ~500 cells/mm²
  • These cells cannot regenerate. When a cell dies, its neighbours simply spread out to cover the gap.
💎

Once density drops below 500 cells/mm², the pump cannot keep up. Fluid floods the stroma, painful epithelial blisters form and vision drops badly. This is Bullous Keratopathy.

🟡 Clinical

Dua's Layer: The 2013 Discovery

In 2013, Professor Harminder Dua described a previously unknown layer sitting between the posterior stroma and Descemet's membrane. It is acellular, about 15μm thick, and surprisingly strong.

💎

DALK Surgery: In the "big bubble" technique for Deep Anterior Lamellar Keratoplasty, surgeons inject air to separate Dua's layer from Descemet's membrane. This lets them replace the diseased stroma while the patient keeps their own healthy endothelium.
