Chapter 12

Strabismus & Ocular Motility Disorders

Eye alignment, cover tests, amblyopia, esotropia, exotropia, restrictive syndromes — clinical diagnosis and surgical management.

UG Level PG Level High-Yield

Definitions & Assessment Methods

UG
▼
  • Orthophoria: Eyes perfectly aligned (no latent or manifest deviation)
  • Heterophoria (latent): Deviation present only when fusion interrupted (cover test reveals it); symptom-free at distance
  • Heterotropia (manifest): Obvious misalignment; eye deviation visible without cover test

Cover Tests (Gold Standard)

  • Cover-Uncover Test: Covers one eye, observe movement of other eye when cover removed (detects fixation preference, strabismic deviation)
  • Alternate Cover Test: Covers each eye alternately (measures total deviation = phoria + tropia)
  • Unilateral Cover Test: Reveals which eye is dominant (fixation with uncovered eye)
Prism Cover Test: After cover test, place prism in front of eye until no movement occurs on cover removal — prism value = magnitude of deviation (diopters/prism diopters).

Amblyopia ("Lazy Eye")

UGPG
▼
  • Definition: Reduced visual acuity not correctable by glasses; due to abnormal visual experience during critical period
  • Sensitive period: Birth to 8 years (peak plasticity 4–6 years)
  • Types:
    • Strabismic: Most common (40–50%); fixation preference → non-fixating eye suppressed
    • Anisometropic: Refractive error difference (e.g., one eye myopic, other hyperopic) → one eye blurred
    • Deprivation: Cataract, corneal opacity, vitreous hemorrhage → stimulus deprivation
    • Refractive: High bilateral refractive error (rare)
  • Treatment (SPECT):
    • Spectacles — correct refractive error first
    • Prisms — correct strabismus
    • Exercises/orthoptics
    • Cyclopentolate penalisation OR Occlusion (patching better eye 1–6 hrs/day)
    • Toxin (botulinum) — temporary paralysis of fixating eye muscles
    • Surgery — realign eyes if patching fails
️ Critical Window: Prognosis worsens after age 8. Therapy less effective after age 12. Early diagnosis = best outcome.

Exotropia, Hypertropia, Restrictive Syndromes

UGPG
▼

Exotropia (Divergent Strabismus)

  • Divergence excess: Deviation larger at distance; treat with base-out prisms or bilateral lateral rectus recession
  • Convergence insufficiency: Deviation worse at near; exercises or base-in prisms
  • Basic exotropia: Equal at distance and near; bilateral medial rectus resection

Hypertropia & CN IV Palsy

  • Superior oblique palsy (CN IV): Head tilt to opposite side (compensatory); Bielschowsky head tilt test POSITIVE (tilting head to affected side improves hypertropia)
  • Treatment: Head posture counseling; surgery (SO tendon lengthening, IR weakening)

Duane's Retraction Syndrome

  • Type I (most common): Abduction failure; normal adduction; eye retracts and narrows on adduction
  • Type II: Adduction failure; abduction normal
  • Type III: Both adduction and abduction limited
  • Pathophysiology: CN VI nucleus aplasia or hypoplasia; aberrant CN III innervation → co-contraction of recti
  • Treatment: Surgery only if cosmetically significant; prisms for diplopia

Brown's Syndrome

  • Feature: Restriction of elevation in adduction (SO tendon sheath thickening/adhesions)
  • Landmark sign: Clicking and widening of palpebral fissure on forced elevation in adduction
  • Congenital vs acquired: Congenital = idiopathic; acquired = post-trauma, surgery, inflammation

Hess Chart & A/V Patterns

PG
▼

Hess Chart (Tangent Screen Test)

  • Uses: Quantify ocular motility; diagnose paralytic vs restrictive strabismus
  • Normal chart: Square-ish field of fixation, symmetrical
  • Paralytic palsy: Chart contracted on side of affected muscle (e.g., CN VI palsy = contracted on lateral gaze)
  • Restrictive: Opposite eye's field contracted (e.g., thyroid eye disease = restricted elevation)

A & V Patterns

  • A pattern: Esotropia increases or exotropia decreases in upgaze; opposite in downgaze
  • V pattern: Exotropia increases or esotropia decreases in upgaze; opposite in downgaze
  • Causes A: Overaction of obliques or underaction of vertical recti
  • Causes V: Underaction of obliques or overaction of vertical recti
  • Treatment: Bifocals, vertical prisms, surgery (oblique surgery)

SPECT Principle — Conservative to Surgical Management

UGPG
▼
SPECT Principle (Sequential Treatment)
S-P-E-C-T-S
Spectacles — Correct refractive error (accommodative ET requires glasses) · Prisms — Reduce deviation, reduce suppression, improve fusion · Exercises/orthoptics — Improve vergence, accommodation · Cyclopentolate penalisation — Blur dominant eye to force fixation on amblyopic eye · Toxin (botulinum) — Temporary muscle paralysis · Surgery — Recession/resection of extraocular muscles when conservative therapy fails

Surgical Principles

  • Recession: Detach muscle origin and reattach farther back → weakens muscle (for overpull)
  • Resection: Shorten muscle by excising a segment → strengthens muscle (for underpull)
  • Dosing: Calculate based on deviation magnitude and type

Diplopia, Suppression, Fusion

UG
▼
  • Monocular diplopia: Persists with one eye closed (corneal, lens, refractive defect — not strabismus)
  • Binocular diplopia: Disappears when one eye covered (indicates strabismus with both eyes open)
  • Suppression: CNS ignores image from deviating eye; develops in childhood strabismus (amblyopia risk); adult onset = no suppression → diplopia
  • Fusion: Binocular single vision; requires normal alignment + normal cortical development
Presentation difference: Childhood-onset strabismus → suppression, no diplopia, amblyopia risk. Adult-onset → no suppression, diplopia (uncomfortable but preserves vision).

MCQ Bank — Strabismus

10 questions · ~20 min
Q1 UG
A 3-year-old child is brought with inward deviation of the right eye since 6 months of age. The angle is large (~50 prism diopters), and the child cross-fixes well. What is the diagnosis and immediate management?
AAccommodative esotropia; prescribe glasses
BInfantile (congenital) esotropia; refer for early surgical correction
CVI nerve palsy; neuroimaging and medical management
DStrabismic amblyopia; start occlusion therapy alone
Correct Answer: B — Infantile esotropia; early surgery
Onset <6 months, large angle, cross-fixation, no hypermetropia correction = infantile ET. Surgery should be done by 6–12 months to establish binocular vision and prevent permanent suppression.
Q2 UG
A 2-year-old with esotropia is found to have +3.00 D hypermetropia. After prescribing glasses, the esotropia fully resolves. What is the mechanism?
AAccommodative esotropia triggered by hypermetropia; glasses relax accommodation and reduce convergence
BGlasses correct the refractive error and improve retinal focus
CGlasses weaken the medial rectus muscle
DSpontaneous resolution unrelated to glasses
Correct Answer: A — Accommodative esotropia
In hypermetropia, accommodation is needed to maintain focus. Accommodation triggers convergence (synkinesis). Glasses allow relaxed accommodation → reduced convergence demand → esotropia resolves.
Q3 PG
A 6-year-old child presents with reduced vision in the left eye (OD: 6/6, OS: 6/36). No refractive error is found on cycloplegic refraction. Cover test shows left eye esotropia. What is the likely diagnosis and FIRST-LINE treatment?
ARefractive amblyopia; prescribe spectacles
BStrabismic amblyopia; start occlusion of right eye (patching)
CDeprivation amblyopia; investigate for corneal opacity
DAnisometropic amblyopia; start cycloplegic penalisation
Correct Answer: B — Strabismic amblyopia; occlusion
Strabismic amblyopia (most common, 40–50% of cases) occurs when fixation preference → suppression of non-fixating eye. Treatment: occlusion of better eye (OD) to force fixation and development of left eye. Child is within critical period (age 8).
Q4 UG
In a patient with duane retraction syndrome Type I, the primary defect is:
AAbsent abduction due to CN VI nucleus aplasia with aberrant CN III fibers
BOveraction of medial rectus
CParalysis of lateral rectus
DPrimary overaction of obliques
Correct Answer: A — CN VI nucleus aplasia with aberrant CN III
Duane syndrome Type I: abduction failure (no CN VI), but adduction normal. Co-innervation of both rectus muscles → palpebral fissure narrows on adduction. Congenital; no surgical treatment unless cosmetically significant.
Q5 PG
A patient with Brown's syndrome (congenital) presents with complaint of vertical diplopia on upgaze. Which clinical finding is PATHOGNOMONIC?
AHypertropia in abduction
BWidening of palpebral fissure on forced elevation in adduction with clicking
CEsotropia in upgaze
DExotropia in downgaze
Correct Answer: B — Widening of fissure with clicking on forced elevation in adduction
Brown's syndrome = SO tendon sheath adhesions → restriction of elevation in adduction. Landmark sign: clicking + widening of palpebral fissure (relaxation of restrictive force when patient attempts forceful elevation in adduction).
Q6 UG
Alternate cover test reveals 10 prism diopters of exotropia at distance and 15 prism diopters at near. Which type of exotropia is this?
ADivergence excess
BBasic exotropia
CConvergence insufficiency
DIntermittent exotropia
Correct Answer: C — Convergence insufficiency
Convergence insufficiency: deviation WORSE at near (15 PD) than distance (10 PD). Treatment: exercises or base-in prisms. Divergence excess = worse at distance. Basic = equal distance/near.
Q7 PG
A Hess chart in a patient with CN III palsy shows:
AEnlarged field of fixation on the affected side
BSymmetrical field (normal Hess)
CContracted field on the affected side (especially downgaze and abduction)
DContracted field on the opposite (normal) side due to compensatory movements
Correct Answer: C — Contracted field on affected side
Paralytic strabismus (CN III palsy) = field contracted on SIDE OF LESION. Restrictive (e.g., thyroid eye disease) = opposite eye's field contracted. Hess chart helps differentiate paralytic vs restrictive.
Q8 UG
Which of the following represents a V pattern in strabismus?
AEsotropia increases in upgaze and exotropia decreases in downgaze
BExotropia increases in upgaze and esotropia decreases in upgaze
CNo change in deviation with vertical gaze
DHypertropia in abduction
Correct Answer: A — Esotropia ↑ in upgaze, Exotropia ↓ in upgaze
V pattern: Esotropia increases or exotropia decreases in upgaze. A pattern: opposite (esotropia increases or exotropia decreases in downgaze). Caused by oblique muscle imbalance.
Q9 PG
In a patient with adult-onset esotropia, why does diplopia occur while a child with childhood-onset esotropia does NOT experience diplopia?
AAdult eye muscles are stiffer and cannot compensate
BChild's brain undergoes suppression of the deviating eye due to cortical plasticity; adult brain cannot suppress
CAdult has worse visual acuity baseline
DChildren have larger pupils and better accommodative response
Correct Answer: B — Suppression in children, not in adults
Childhood strabismus: CNS suppresses image from deviating eye → no diplopia but amblyopia risk. Adult-onset: no plastic suppression → diplopia (uncomfortable but vision preserved). Adult prognosis better; child prognosis needs urgent intervention.
Q10 UG
The SPECT principle in strabismus management emphasizes treatment in what order?
ASurgery first, then glasses, then prisms
BSpectacles → Prisms → Exercises → Cyclopentolate/Occlusion → Toxin → Surgery
COcclusion therapy followed immediately by surgery
DBotulinum toxin before glasses
Correct Answer: B — Sequential SPECT approach
SPECT: Start conservative, progress to surgical if needed. Most accommodative ET corrected by glasses alone. Amblyopia treated with occlusion. Surgery reserved for when conservative measures insufficient.

University / MBBS Exams
University Final MBBS
Describe the cover test, alternate cover test, and their interpretation in a case of manifest esotropia. What is the diagnostic value of prism cover test? (5 marks)
Cover test: detects fixation preference. Alternate cover test: measures total deviation (phoria + tropia). Prism cover test: prism strength at which pupil stops moving = magnitude of deviation in prism diopters. Helps quantify and track progression.

Definitions & Key Tests

  • Orthophoria: Perfect alignment
  • Heterophoria: Latent deviation (fusion breaks it)
  • Heterotropia: Manifest deviation (obvious)
  • Cover-uncover: Detects which eye fixates (preference)
  • Alternate cover: Measures total deviation (phoria + tropia)
  • Prism cover: Quantifies deviation in prism diopters

Amblyopia

  • Definition: Reduced VA not correctable by glasses
  • Sensitive period: Birth–8 yr (peak 4–6 yr)
  • Most common: Strabismic (40–50%)
  • Treatment: SPECT principle (sequential)
  • Prognosis: Better <8 yr; poor >12 yr

Esotropia Types

  • Infantile (congenital):<6mo, large angle, cross-fixation → surgery 6–12 mo
  • Accommodative: Hypermetropia-triggered → glasses correct fully
  • Non-accommodative: No hypermetropia → surgery needed
  • VI nerve palsy: Esotropia worsens in abduction

Other Strabismus

  • Exotropia: Divergence excess (worse at distance); Convergence insufficiency (worse at near); Basic (equal)
  • CN IV palsy: Hypertropia; Bielschowsky head tilt test POSITIVE
  • Duane syndrome: Type I = abduction failure; fissure narrows on adduction
  • Brown's syndrome: SO adhesion; clicking + fissure widens on forced elevation in adduction
