Definitions

  • Blindness (WHO): VA < 3/60 in better eye with correction
  • Blindness (India, under Prevention of Blindness Act): VA < 6/60 in better eye with correction
  • Low vision: VA < 6/18 but ≥ 3/60 with correction; functional limitation but not totally blind
  • Visual field defect: VF < 10° around fixation also qualifies as blindness (even if VA normal)

Priority Order (India)

  1. Cataract: 35% (most preventable; cataract surgery efficacy high)
  2. Uncorrected refractive error: 21% (glasses/contact lens simple fix)
  3. Posterior segment disorders: Glaucoma (8%), ARMD (8%), DR (5%)
  4. Corneal blindness: 4% (from trauma, infection, vitamin A deficiency)

VISION 2020 & National Programmes

UG
▼
VISION 2020: WHO + IAPB global initiative (1999). Slogan: "Right to Sight". Goal: eliminate avoidable blindness by 2020 (extended beyond 2020).

National Programme for Control of Blindness (NPCB) — 1976

  • Launched: 1976; India's first organized eye health programme
  • Objectives:
    • Cataract: strengthening surgical services, mobile eye camps
    • Corneal transplant: tissue banks, keratoplasty promotion
    • School eye health: screening, refraction services
    • Low vision: rehabilitation services for partially sighted
  • Target: Reduce blindness prevalence to 0.3% (currently ~1% in India)
  • Current Status: Expansion to include screening for glaucoma, DR, ARMD

Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK) — 2013

  • For ages 0–18 years; comprehensive health screening
  • 4D approach: Defects, Diseases, Deficiencies, Developmental delays
  • Eye health component: screening for refractive error, strabismus, cataract, corneal opacity

Vitamin A Supplementation (WHO/India Schedule)

  • High-dose supplementation: 1 lakh IU (100,000 IU) at 9 months of age
  • Every 6 months until 5 years: 2 lakh IU (200,000 IU) doses
  • Pregnant/lactating women: 10,000 IU daily or 2 lakh IU at 6 weeks postpartum (if deficient)
  • Measles vaccination: Vitamin A supplementation at time of vaccination (synergistic protection)

Causes of Childhood Blindness (India)

  • Vitamin A deficiency: Leading preventable cause in developing countries
  • Congenital cataract: From intrauterine infection (TORCH: rubella, toxoplasmosis)
  • Measles keratitis: From corneal ulceration (vitamin A deficiency + measles co-infection)
  • Ophthalmia neonatorum: From untreated maternal STI (N. gonorrhoeae, C. trachomatis); prevented with topical antibiotics (Credé's prophylaxis)

Trachoma Control — SAFE Strategy

UG
▼
SAFE Strategy (WHO)
S—A—F—E
Surgery (trichiasis correction) · Antibiotics (azithromycin) · Facial cleanliness · Environmental improvement

Components

  • Surgery: Trichiasis (inturned lashes) → entropion repair; prevents keratitis and scarring
  • Antibiotics: Azithromycin (single dose or 3-day course) for active infection (TF — trachomatous inflammation-follicular); treats Chlamydia trachomatis
  • Facial cleanliness: Education on hygiene, water access, handwashing (reduces transmission)
  • Environmental improvement: Sanitation, access to clean water, health education

Epidemiology

  • Leading infectious cause of blindness globally (though decreasing)
  • More common in low-income, water-scarce regions (sub-Saharan Africa, parts of Asia)
  • Preventable and treatable if caught early

School Eye Health Programme

UG
▼

Screening Protocol

  • Tool: Snellen chart (distance vision) at 6 metres
  • Referral criteria: VA < 6/9 in either eye on screening chart
  • Refractive error correction: Pinhole test to confirm refractive error vs other pathology
  • Action: Refer for detailed ophthalmology assessment; prescribe glasses if needed

Goals

  • Early detection of refractive error (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism)
  • Identification of ocular disease (cataract, strabismus, colour blindness)
  • Prevention of amblyopia (lazy eye) through early correction
  • Reducing dropout from school due to uncorrected refractive error

Blindness Certification & Disability Grading (India)

PG
▼

Blindness Criteria (Prevention of Blindness Act)

  • VA < 3/60 or VF < 10° around fixation: 75% disability (severe blindness)
  • VA < 6/60 or VF < 20°: 40% disability (moderate visual impairment)
  • Certification required for government benefits, vocational rehabilitation, disability allowance

Criteria for Certification

  • Visual acuity tested with best correction (glasses/contact lens)
  • Both eyes assessed; disability graded on better eye
  • Visual fields (if applicable) measured formally
  • Ophthalmologist certifies permanence (unlikely to improve)

Government Support (India)

  • Blind person monthly assistance (varies by state)
  • Income tax benefits
  • Vocational training and rehabilitation services
  • Low vision rehabilitation

MCQ Bank — Community Ophthalmology

Q1 UG
According to WHO 2023, what percentage of the 2.2 billion visually impaired people have preventable blindness?
A~1 billion (45%)
B~2 billion (90%)
C~500 million (22%)
D~100 million (5%)
Correct Answer: A — ~1 billion (45%)
WHO 2023 data: 2.2 billion visually impaired; 1 billion have preventable blindness (from cataract, refractive error, corneal opacity). This highlights global disparity in access to eye care.
Q2 UG
The leading preventable cause of blindness in India is:
AUncorrected refractive error (21%)
BCataract (35%)
CGlaucoma (8%)
DCorneal opacity (4%)
Correct Answer: B — Cataract (35%)
Cataract is the single largest cause (35%), followed by uncorrected refractive error (21%). Both are highly preventable: cataract by surgery, refractive error by correction (glasses/contact lens).
Q3 UG
According to the Prevention of Blindness Act (India), blindness is defined as VA < ___ in the better eye with correction:
A6/60
B6/36
C3/60
D2/60
Correct Answer: C — 3/60 (or VF < 10° around fixation)
India uses VA < 3/60 (stricter than WHO's < 3/60) or visual field < 10° around fixation for blindness. This allows for 75% disability grading. VA < 6/60 = 40% disability.
Q4 UG
The "XN" stage of xerophthalmia represents:
ANight blindness (scotopic vision impairment)
BBitot's spots
CCorneal scarring
DConjunctival xerosis
Correct Answer: A — Night blindness
XN (Xerophthalmia Night blindness) is the earliest stage of vitamin A deficiency; reversible with supplementation. Rod photoreceptors require vitamin A (retinol → retinal → rhodopsin). Early sign; good prognostic value for intervention.
Q5 UG
X3B stage of xerophthalmia (corneal ulceration ≥ 1/3 cornea) carries which prognosis?
AFully reversible with vitamin A
BOften permanent corneal scarring and blindness
CResolves with topical antibiotics
DRequires glasses for correction
Correct Answer: B — Often permanent corneal scarring and blindness
X3B (extensive corneal ulceration ≥ 1/3) represents severe melting; even with high-dose vitamin A, scarring (leukoma) and blindness often result. X3A (< 1/3) has better prognosis. Prevention (early supplementation) is key.
Q6 UG
Vitamin A supplementation schedule (WHO/India) for infants 9 months – 5 years:
A10,000 IU once yearly
B50,000 IU monthly
C1 lakh IU at 9 mo; then 2 lakh IU every 6 mo until 5 yr
D5,000 IU daily
Correct Answer: C — 1 lakh IU at 9 mo; then 2 lakh IU every 6 mo until 5 yr
High-dose supplementation: 100,000 IU (1 lakh) once at 9 months, then 200,000 IU (2 lakh) every 6 months through age 5. This is WHO standard; also given at measles vaccination. Cost-effective prevention of xerophthalmia.
Q7 UG
The "SAFE strategy" for trachoma control stands for:
ASurgery, Azathioprine, Fluids, Eyedrops
BSurgery, Antibiotics (azithromycin), Facial cleanliness, Environmental improvement
CScreening, Antituberculous drugs, Filtration, Education
DScleral buckling, Aspiration, Fluorescence, Examination
Correct Answer: B — Surgery, Antibiotics, Facial cleanliness, Environmental improvement
SAFE is WHO's comprehensive trachoma control strategy: Surgery for trichiasis, Azithromycin for active infection, Facial cleanliness (handwashing, water access), Environmental improvements (sanitation).
Q8 UG
School eye health screening uses ___ chart; refers VA < ___:
ASnellen; 6/9
BLandolt C; 6/12
CETDRS; 6/6
DJaeger; 6/18
Correct Answer: A — Snellen chart; 6/9
School screening: Snellen chart at 6 metres distance. Refer if VA < 6/9 (fails to read line). Pinhole test helps distinguish refractive error from pathology. Early detection prevents amblyopia and school dropout.
Q9 PG
NPCB (National Programme for Control of Blindness) was launched in which year?
A1960
B1970
C1976
D1990
Correct Answer: C — 1976
NPCB was launched in 1976; focuses on cataract surgery, corneal transplant, school eye health, and low vision services. Target: reduce blindness prevalence to 0.3% (currently ~1%).
Q10 PG
Bitot's spots (X1B stage xerophthalmia) are:
ARetinal crystal deposits
BCorneal ulcers
CIris heterochromia
DFoamy white spots on temporal conjunctiva; reversible
Correct Answer: D — Foamy white spots on temporal conjunctiva; reversible
Bitot's spots are pathognomonic foamy white spots (desquamated epithelium) on the temporal bulbar conjunctiva. Reversible with vitamin A. Important clinical sign of vitamin A deficiency; used for epidemiological surveys.

University / MBBS Exams
University Final MBBS
Describe the SAFE strategy for trachoma control. List the components and explain the role of each. (6 marks)
S = Surgery (trichiasis correction); A = Antibiotics (azithromycin for active infection); F = Facial cleanliness (hygiene, water); E = Environmental improvement (sanitation). Comprehensive approach reduces transmission and prevents blindness.
University Final MBBS
Enumerate the stages of xerophthalmia and discuss the reversibility and prognosis at each stage. (8 marks)
XN (night blindness) → X1A (conjunctival xerosis) → X1B (Bitot's spots) → X2 (corneal xerosis) → X3A (ulceration <1/3) → X3B (ulceration ≥1/3) → XF (xerophthalmic fundus) → XS (scar). Early stages (XN–X2) reversible; X3B onwards often irreversible scarring/blindness. Prevention (vitamin A supplementation) key.

Global Burden (WHO 2023)

  • 2.2 billion visually impaired globally
  • ~1 billion cases preventable
  • Top causes: Cataract (35%), Uncorrected RE (21%), Glaucoma (8%), ARMD (8%), DR (5%)
  • In India: Cataract 35%, Uncorrected RE 21%, Posterior segment 20%

Blindness Definitions

  • WHO: VA < 3/60 in better eye
  • India (PBF): VA < 6/60 or VF < 10° = 75% disability; VA < 6/60 = 40% disability
  • Low vision: VA < 6/18 but ≥ 3/60

Xerophthalmia Stages

  • XN: Night blindness (earliest, reversible)
  • X1A/X1B: Conjunctival xerosis, Bitot's spots (reversible)
  • X2: Corneal xerosis (reversible early)
  • X3A/X3B: Corneal ulceration (<1/3 or ≥1/3; scarring risk)
  • XF/XS: Xerophthalmic fundus/scar (irreversible)

Key Programmes

  • VISION 2020: WHO + IAPB; eliminate avoidable blindness
  • NPCB: India 1976; cataract, corneal transplant, school screening, low vision
  • RBSK: 0–18 yr; 4D: defects, diseases, deficiencies, delays
  • Vit A: 1 lakh IU at 9 mo; 2 lakh IU every 6 mo until 5 yr
