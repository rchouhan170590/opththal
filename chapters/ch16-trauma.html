Chapter 16

Ocular Trauma

Blunt and penetrating injury, chemical and radiation burns, intraocular foreign bodies — emergency management and prognostication.

UG Level PG Level High-Yield

Mechanical Trauma — Closed Globe (Blunt)

UGPG
▼
Fig 16.1 — Closed globe blunt injury features

Common Injuries

  • Subconjunctival haemorrhage: Superficial; no vision threat; self-limiting
  • Hyphema: Blood in anterior chamber; graded 0–IV (total—entire AC filled with RBCs); risk of secondary glaucoma (ghost cells, microthrombi); treatment: bed rest, eye shield, avoid aspirin, elevate head to 30°; CAI if IOP raised
  • Commotio retinae (Berlin's oedema): Whitening at macula; photoreceptor disruption; usually resolves in 1–2 weeks; vision recovers
  • Lens subluxation/dislocation: Zonular damage; phacodonesis (lens wobbles); if dislocated: aphakic; risk of phthisis
  • Choroidal rupture: White crescent-shaped breaks; vision usually normal (if not at macula)
  • Vitreous haemorrhage: Can obscure retinal view; may absorb; intravitreal surgery if dense
️ Hyphema Risk: Total hyphema (Grade IV) has worst prognosis; secondary glaucoma in 50%+.

Management

  • Emergency: Do NOT manipulate; shield eye; IV fluids, NPO
  • Tetanus prophylaxis as per guidelines
  • Primary repair: Microsurgical wound closure (nylon, absorbable); anterior chamber reformation if needed
  • Intravitreal antibiotics: Ceftazidime + amikacin (if globe ruptured)
  • Monitor: Endophthalmitis risk (5–13%), secondary glaucoma, retinal detachment

Intraocular Foreign Body (IOFB)

PG
▼

Diagnosis & Imaging

  • X-ray orbit: Metallic IOFB (iron, steel, copper) radio-opaque
  • B-scan ultrasound: Non-metallic FB; assess posterior segment
  • CT orbit: Most sensitive; high-resolution; safe with any material
  • Avoid MRI: Risk of FB movement (ferromagnetic materials)
Metals in IOFB — Toxicity
CHIMP
Copper (chalcosis) · High toxicity · Iron (siderosis) · Most common · Poor prognosis

Copper Toxicity (Chalcosis)

  • Sunflower cataract: Anterior subcapsular; characteristic radiating pattern
  • Kayser-Fleischer-like ring: Greenish-brown ring at Descemet's membrane
  • Green-ish discoloration of iris and cornea

Iron Toxicity (Siderosis)

  • Heterochromia: Iris browning (rust deposit)
  • Anterior chamber: Brown staining
  • Retinal damage: Poor prognosis; vision loss even with removal
  • RPE atrophy, photoreceptor degeneration

Management

  • Remove ASAP to prevent toxicity; magnet-assisted or forceps removal
  • Anterior FB: slit lamp extraction
  • Posterior FB: pars plana vitrectomy for removal
  • Monitor post-removal for cataract, glaucoma, retinal complications

Sympathetic Ophthalmia (SO)

PG
▼
Definition: Bilateral granulomatous panuveitis after penetrating trauma (or surgery) to one eye. Rare (0.01–0.03% of penetrating injuries) but sight-threatening.

Clinical Features

  • Timeline: Latent period 2 weeks to months (range: days to years)
  • Inciting eye: Often had poor prognosis; "exciting eye"
  • Sympathizing eye: Bilateral panuveitis (anterior + posterior); photopsia, floaters, VA drop
  • Granulomatous reaction: Keratic precipitates, iris nodules, posterior synechiae
  • Posterior involvement: Vitritis, optic disc swelling, cotton-wool spots
  • Dalen-Fuchs nodules: Nodules at RPE-Bruch's interface (characteristic, but rare)

Management

  • High-dose corticosteroids: Systemic prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day, taper slowly; may need months
  • Immunosuppressants: Azathioprine, mycophenolate, or tacrolimus if steroid-dependent or resistant
  • Enucleation of exciting eye: Traditionally within 2 weeks of SO diagnosis; now controversial — many achieve remission with steroids alone
  • Prognosis: Fair with early steroids; 60–80% achieve useful vision

Acute Management (First 20–30 minutes)

  • IMMEDIATE copious irrigation: Normal saline, continuous flow for 20–30 min; check final pH ≥ 7.0 before stopping
  • Do NOT use buffers if no saline available — tap water is acceptable
  • Remove particles: Especially lime/plaster deposits (use forceps, jeweler's loupe)

Subsequent Management

  • Topical agents: Prednisolone 1% hourly (reduce over weeks), vitamin C (ascorbic acid) 10% TID, tetracycline 1% to prevent symblepharon
  • EDTA: Topical for calcium deposits (lime injuries)
  • Grade III–IV: Limbal stem cell transplant (LSCT) if no epithelialization by 2–3 weeks
  • Cicatricial changes: Symblepharon release, lid margin reconstruction (if needed)

Lime (Calcium Hydroxide) Injuries

  • Whitewash/plaster particles cause severe alkali burn
  • Key step: Remove all visible particles FIRST, then irrigate (prevents continued release)

Radiation Injuries

UG
▼

UV (Ultraviolet) — Acute Keratitis

  • Exposure: Welder's arc, sun exposure (unprotected), snow glare
  • Onset: 6–12 hours post-exposure (latency period)
  • Keratitis: Superficial punctate epithelial damage; diffuse corneal erosion
  • Symptoms: Intense pain, photophobia, tearing, blepharospasm
  • Treatment: Topical anesthetics (lubricants), eye patching (bandage contact lens), topical NSAIDs (diclofenac), antibiotics; resolves in 24–48 hr

Infrared (IR) — Glass-blower's Cataract

  • Exposure: Glass-blowing, furnace workers (radiant heat)
  • Cataract type: Posterior subcapsular (PSC); unique vacuole pattern
  • Latency: months to years

X-ray / Ionizing Radiation

  • Cataract: Posterior subcapsular; latency several years
  • Risk increases with cumulative dose (occupational exposure)

Thermal Burns

UG
▼

Mechanisms & Sites

  • Eyelid burns: Most common ocular injury from flames; thermal damage to skin; severe if involving lid margin
  • Corneal epithelial loss: Direct contact with hot surfaces; severe pain

Management

  • Acute: Lubrication (artificial tears, ointment), topical antibiotics, eye shield
  • Pain control: Analgesics; topical NSAIDs OK
  • Lid reconstruction: If margin involved or severe contracture; may need Z-plasty, graft
  • Monitor for secondary glaucoma, cataracts (chronic)

MCQ Bank — Ocular Trauma

10 questions · ~25 min
Q1 UG
Total hyphema (Grade IV) carries what risk of secondary glaucoma?
A5–10%
B50%+
C2–3%
D30–40%
Correct Answer: B — 50%+
Grade IV (total) hyphema has the highest risk of secondary glaucoma from ghost cells, RBC debris, and microthrombi obstructing trabecular meshwork. Treatment: bed rest, avoid aspirin, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors.
Q2 UG
Which chemical is MORE damaging to the eye than acid?
ASulfuric acid
BAlkali (lime, ammonia)
CHydrochloric acid
DNitric acid
Correct Answer: B — Alkali
Alkali penetrates deeper into corneal stroma than acid. It causes saponification of lipid membranes → continuing damage even after exposure ends. Acid denatures proteins and creates a coagulum that limits penetration.
Q3 PG
Commotio retinae (Berlin's oedema) at the macula after blunt trauma typically:
ACauses permanent vision loss
BRequires emergency vitrectomy
CResolves in 1–2 weeks with vision recovery
DIndicates retinal detachment
Correct Answer: C — Resolves in 1–2 weeks with vision recovery
Commotio retinae (photoreceptor disruption) is usually self-limiting. The white appearance from outer retinal disruption typically clears over 1–2 weeks, with good functional recovery unless accompanied by choroidal rupture at the macula.
Q4 UG
In a perforating eye injury, which finding is MOST likely?
ALow IOP, peaked pupil, visible uvea
BElevated IOP, normal pupil, no uvea
CNormal IOP, RAPD, clear cornea
DHyphema only, normal IOP
Correct Answer: A — Low IOP, peaked pupil, visible uvea
Perforating injuries have aqueous leak → low IOP, pulled/peaked pupil, gaping wound revealing uvea. Penetrating (entry only) may have variable findings. Emergency repair needed.
Q5 PG
Copper IOFB causes which type of cataract?
AAnterior subcapsular
BSunflower cataract (anterior, radiating)
CPosterior subcapsular
DNuclear sclerosis
Correct Answer: B — Sunflower cataract
Copper toxicity (chalcosis) causes a characteristic sunflower cataract with anterior subcapsular radiating opacities. Also causes Kayser-Fleischer-like ring and green iris discoloration.
Q6 PG
Iron IOFB (siderosis) produces which finding?
AGreen-brown ring at Descemet's (Kayser-Fleischer)
BSunflower lens opacity
CHeterochromia iridis (iris browning) + poor retinal prognosis
DAnterior white opacity only
Correct Answer: C — Heterochromia iridis + poor retinal prognosis
Iron deposits cause rust-coloured changes in iris (heterochromia), anterior chamber browning, and RPE atrophy → poor prognosis even with IOFB removal. Copper causes sunflower cataract and Kayser-Fleischer ring.
Q7 UG
How long after chemical irrigation should you check pH to confirm adequate decontamination?
AAfter 20–30 min continuous irrigation; target pH ≥ 7.0
BAfter 5 min only
COnce, at start
DNot necessary; visual inspection suffices
Correct Answer: A — After 20–30 min; target pH ≥ 7.0
gold standard: Copious irrigation for 20–30 minutes, then pH check at surface (conjunctiva/fornices). Final pH must be ≥ 7.0. Continue irrigation if pH remains abnormal.
Q8 PG
Sympathetic ophthalmia develops with what latency after exciting eye penetrating injury?
AAlways within 5 days
B2 weeks to months (range days to years)
CNever before 1 year
DOnly acutely (same day)
Correct Answer: B — 2 weeks to months (range days to years)
SO latency is highly variable: typically 2 weeks to months, but can range from days to years. Most occur within first 3 months. Always consider SO in bilateral anterior uveitis post-trauma.
Q9 UG
UV keratitis ("welder's flash") develops with what latency?
AImmediately (0–1 hour)
B2–3 hours
C24 hours
D6–12 hours
Correct Answer: D — 6–12 hours
UV keratitis has a characteristic latency of 6–12 hours (sometimes 12–24 hr) post-exposure. Intense corneal pain, photophobia, blepharospasm follow. Treatment: patching, NSAIDs, lubricants; resolves in 24–48 hours.
Q10 PG
In Roper-Hall Grade IV chemical burn, what is the prognosis and key intervention?
AExcellent; topical antibiotics only
BGood with steroids alone
CPoor; requires high-dose steroids, vitamin C, LSCT if no epithelialization in 2–3 weeks
DModerate; patch and bandage CL
Correct Answer: C — Poor; requires high-dose steroids, Vit C, LSCT
Roper-Hall Grade IV (total limbal ischaemia, complete corneal opacity) is the worst category. Management: high-dose topical steroids, ascorbic acid, tetracycline to prevent symblepharon, and limbal stem cell transplantation (LSCT) if epithelialization fails by 2–3 weeks.

University / MBBS Exams
University Final MBBS
Describe the management of a patient with total hyphema (Grade IV) post-trauma. (5 marks)
Bed rest, eye shield, elevate head 30°, avoid aspirin, monitor IOP, use CAI if IOP raised, consider anterior chamber drainage if persistent high IOP (>35 mmHg) + corneal changes
University Final MBBS
A 45-year-old presents 6 weeks post-penetrating injury with bilateral anterior uveitis, photopsia, VF defects. Discuss diagnosis, features, and management. (8 marks)
Diagnosis: Sympathetic ophthalmia. Features: bilateral panuveitis, granulomatous KP, Dalen-Fuchs nodules, vitritis, optic disc swelling. Management: High-dose systemic prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day ± immunosuppressants; controversial enucleation of exciting eye.

Hyphema Grading

  • Grade 0 = Microscopic RBCs only
  • Grade I = <1/4 AC filled
  • Grade II = 1/4 to 1/2 AC
  • Grade III = 1/2 to full AC
  • Grade IV = Total (100%) AC
  • Grade IV risk: 50%+ secondary glaucoma

Chemical Burn (Roper-Hall)

  • Grade I: Epithelial loss only → Excellent
  • Grade II: Haze + <1/3 limbus → Good
  • Grade III: Opacity + >1/3 limbus → Guarded
  • Grade IV: Total opacity + total limbal ischaemia → Poor
  • Alkali > Acid: Penetrates deeper
  • Irrigate 20–30 min; check pH ≥ 7.0

IOFB Metals

  • Copper (Chalcosis): Sunflower cataract, Kayser-Fleischer ring, green iris
  • Iron (Siderosis): Heterochromia iridis, RPE atrophy, poor prognosis
  • Imaging: CT safest; avoid MRI (ferromagnetic risk)
  • Remove ASAP to prevent toxicity

Classic Presentations

  • Commotio retinae: White macula → recovers in 1–2 weeks
  • SO latency: 2 weeks–months (range days–years)
  • UV keratitis: 6–12 hr latency; resolves in 24–48 hr
  • Perforating: Low IOP, peaked pupil, visible uvea
  • Penetrating: Variable IOP, may have RAPD
