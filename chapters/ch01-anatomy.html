Chapter 1

Anatomy & Physiology of the Eye

Structural organisation, blood supply, visual pathway, aqueous humour dynamics — the foundation for all clinical ophthalmology.

UG Level PG Level High-Yield

Introduction

UG
▼
Key Fact: The eyeball is an AP diameter of ~24 mm (axial length). Every 1 mm increase = ~3D of myopia.
  • Adult eyeball: axial length 24 mm, horizontal 23.5 mm, vertical 23 mm
  • Weight: ~7 g; Volume: ~6.5 mL
  • Three coats: Fibrous (sclera + cornea) → Vascular (uvea: choroid + CB + iris) → Nervous (retina)
  • Three chambers: Anterior (aqueous), Posterior (aqueous), Vitreous

Retina — Layers & Macula

UGPG
▼
Fig 1.2 — Ten layers of the retina

Macula & Fovea

  • Macula lutea: Area of highest visual acuity; ~5 mm diameter, centered ~3.5 mm temporal to disc
  • Fovea centralis: Central depression; diameter ~1.5 mm; contains only cones (no rods)
  • Foveola: ~0.35 mm; thinnest part; only cone inner segments, no capillaries → avascular zone
  • Optic disc: 1.5 mm, blind spot; no photoreceptors; physiological cup:disc ratio ≤0.3
️ Exam Trap: At the macula, GCL is 8–10 layers thick (not 1 like elsewhere). At fovea, only cones are present — no rods, no capillaries.

Visual Pathway

UGPG
▼
Fig 1.3 — Visual pathway from retina to cortex

Key Anatomical Points

  • Optic chiasm: Nasal (crossing) fibres cross; temporal fibres stay ipsilateral
  • LGN (lateral geniculate nucleus): 6 layers — layers 1, 2 = magnocellular; 3, 4, 5, 6 = parvocellular
  • Meyer's loop: Inferior fibres of optic radiation loop into temporal lobe → lesion → contralateral superior quadrantanopia ("pie in the sky")
  • Baum's loop: Superior fibres → parietal lobe → lesion → inferior quadrantanopia ("pie on floor")
  • Visual cortex: Brodmann area 17 (striate), 18 & 19 (parastriate); macular area occupies posterior pole (macular sparing in PCA territory infarcts)

MCQ Bank — Anatomy & Physiology

10 questions · ~20 min
Q1 UG
Which layer of the cornea CANNOT regenerate after injury?
AEpithelium
BBowman's layer
CStroma
DDescemet's membrane
Correct Answer: B — Bowman's layer
  • Bowman's layer is acellular — once destroyed it forms a scar. No regenerative capacity.
  • Epithelium regenerates well (stem cells at limbus).
  • Stroma regenerates partially.
  • Descemet's membrane is regenerated by endothelium.
Q2 UG
The fovea centralis of the retina is characterised by which of the following?
APresence of rods only
BRich capillary network for high metabolism
CCones only with no capillaries (avascular zone)
DGanglion cell layer is 8–10 cells thick
Correct Answer: C — Cones only with no capillaries
Foveola is avascular — nutrients come from choriocapillaris. Contains only cone inner segments. All other retinal layers are displaced laterally (Henle's fibre layer). The GCL being 8–10 layers thick is a feature of the macula, not the foveola.
Q3 PG
A lesion in Meyer's loop (temporal lobe portion of optic radiation) produces which visual field defect?
AIpsilateral monocular visual loss
BContralateral inferior quadrantanopia
CContralateral superior quadrantanopia ("pie in the sky")
DBitemporal hemianopia
Correct Answer: C — Contralateral superior quadrantanopia
  • Meyer's loop carries inferior visual field fibres (lower retina = upper visual field).
  • Temporal lobe lesion → "pie in the sky" = contralateral superior quadrantanopia.
  • Parietal lobe (Baum's loop, superior fibres) → contralateral inferior quadrantanopia = "pie on the floor".
  • Optic chiasm → bitemporal hemianopia.
Q4 UG
Normal IOP range and the rate of aqueous humor production are, respectively:
A10–21 mmHg; 2 µL/min
B15–30 mmHg; 5 µL/min
C10–21 mmHg; 0.5 µL/min
D12–25 mmHg; 2 µL/min
Correct Answer: A — 10–21 mmHg; 2 µL/min
Normal IOP: 10–21 mmHg (Goldmann tonometer). Aqueous produced at ~2 µL/min by ciliary epithelium. Drains 85% trabecular, 15% uveoscleral.
Q5 PG
Which extraocular muscle is responsible for intorsion when the eye is in primary position?
ASuperior rectus
BSuperior oblique
CInferior rectus
DInferior oblique
Correct Answer: B — Superior oblique
Superior oblique (CN IV): primary action = intorsion; secondary = depression and abduction. Inferior oblique: extorsion, elevation, abduction. Superior rectus: elevation, intorsion, adduction.

Answer: A, C, D — Fovea has only cones, is avascular, and is the thinnest area.

University / MBBS Exams
University Final MBBS
Enumerate the layers of the cornea. Add a note on the clinical significance of corneal endothelium. (5 marks)
Layers: Epithelium → Bowman's → Stroma → Descemet's → Endothelium. Clinical: Endothelial cell density <500/mm² → bullous keratopathy; targeted in DSAEK/DMEK surgery.

Must-Know Numbers

  • Axial length = 24 mm (1 mm change = 3D)
  • Normal IOP = 10–21 mmHg
  • Aqueous production = 2 µL/min
  • Corneal diameter = 12 mm (H), 11 mm (V)
  • Optic disc diameter = 1.5 mm
  • Normal C:D ratio ≤ 0.3–0.4
  • Foveola = 0.35 mm (avascular)

Classic Exam Facts

  • Bowman's layer: cannot regenerate → scar
  • Endothelium: pumps fluid; no regen in humans
  • Fovea: only cones, no capillaries
  • LR6 SO4 rest CN3 — nerve to EOM
  • Meyer's loop → "pie in the sky"
  • Nasal fibres cross at chiasm
  • Cilioretinal artery (20%) = spares vision in CRAO

Mnemonics

  • Cornea layers: E B S D E
  • EOM nerve: LR₆SO₄ rest CN3
  • Retinal layers (inside→out): NFL → GCL → IPL → INL → OPL → ONL → OLM → PR → RPE
  • Macular area = 5 mm; Fovea = 1.5 mm; Foveola = 0.35 mm

For PG Studentss

  • LGN: layers 1,2 magnocellular; 3–6 parvocellular
  • Area 17 = primary visual cortex (striate)
  • Macular sparing: posterior cerebral artery territory infarct
  • Henle's fibre layer = outer plexiform at macula
  • Müller cells = structural support across retina
