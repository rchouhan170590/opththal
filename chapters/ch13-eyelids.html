Chapter 13

Diseases of the Eyelids

Ptosis, entropion, ectropion, blepharitis, chalazion, lid tumours — anatomy, classification, and surgical management.

Eyelid Anatomy — Lamellae & Innervation

UG
▼

Two-Lamella Concept

  • Anterior lamella: Skin (epidermis) + orbicularis oculi muscle (CN VII innervated)
  • Posterior lamella: Tarsal plate (fibrous skeleton) + conjunctiva; levator palpebrae superioris (CN III) attaches to superior tarsus; Müller's muscle (sympathetic) assists levator

Key Structures

  • Tarsal plates: Upper = 9–10 mm height; Lower = 4–5 mm; contain meibomian glands (modified sebaceous)
  • Levator palpebrae superioris (LPS): Primary elevator of upper lid; CN III innervated; normal length 40–45 mm; insertion on superior tarsal border and skin (aponeurosis)
  • Müller's muscle: Sympathetic innervated; provides 2–3 mm additional elevation
  • Orbicularis oculi: CN VII (facial nerve); three parts: orbital, palpebral, lacrimal; responsible for lid closure and blinking
Clinical Pearl: Anterior lamella problems (skin, orbicularis) → ectropion/entropion; Posterior lamella problems (LPS, conjunctiva) → ptosis/entropion.

Ptosis Grading (Levator Function)

  • Normal: LF ≥12 mm
  • Good: LF 8–12 mm
  • Moderate: LF 5–7 mm
  • Poor: LF <4 mm
Treatment Options by LF
LF ≥8 mm: Levator resection. LF 4–8 mm: Fasanella-Servat or levator resection. LF <4 mm: Frontalis sling (suspension)

Lid Tumours — Benign & Malignant

PG
▼

Malignant Lid Tumours

  • Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) — Most common malignant lid tumour (~80%)
    • Location: Lower lid medial canthal area (90%)
    • Appearance: "Rodent ulcer" (central ulceration, rolled margin, telangiectasia)
    • Treatment: Mohs micrographic surgery (highest cure rate, tissue sparing)
  • Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)
    • Less common; more aggressive than BCC; higher metastatic potential
    • Worse prognosis if involves levator
  • Sebaceous gland carcinoma — DANGEROUS ("Great Imitator")
    • Location: Upper lid (meibomian gland origin)
    • Masquerades as chalazion → often diagnosed late
    • Skip areas (non-contiguous spread along lid)
    • Poor prognosis if intraepithelial spread present
    • Treatment: Full-thickness lid resection, frozen section for margins
  • Melanoma
    • Rare but highly malignant
    • Often presents with ptosis or bleeding lash

Benign Lid Lesions

  • Nevus, xanthelasma, milia — Cosmetic removal

Lid Retraction & Lagophthalmos

UG
▼

Lid Retraction

  • Thyroid eye disease (TED / Graves' ophthalmopathy):
    • Dalrymple's sign: Upper lid retraction (wide palpebral fissure)
    • von Graefe's sign: Slower descent of upper lid on downgaze (lid lag)
    • Collier's sign: Retraction of upper lid on downgaze (sympathetic overactivity)

Lagophthalmos (Incomplete Lid Closure)

  • CN VII palsy (Bell's palsy): Orbicularis paralysis → inability to close eye
  • Consequences: Exposure keratopathy, corneal erosion, ulceration
  • Treatment: Lubricants, protective eyeglasses, tape/bandage; gold weight implant or levator disinsertion (upper lid); taping, botulinum to antagonist, crutch glasses (temporary)

MCQ Bank — Eyelid Diseases

10 questions · ~20 min
Q1 UG
A 65-year-old man presents with bilateral ptosis worse at the end of the day, without diplopia or pupillary involvement. Levator function is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
ACongenital ptosis
BCN III palsy
CMyasthenia gravis
DHorner syndrome
Correct Answer: C — Myasthenia gravis
Bilateral ptosis + fatigue (worse EOD) + normal levator function = myasthenic weakness. CN III would have reduced LF and may involve pupil. Congenital is childhood. Horner's doesn't show fatigue pattern.
Q2 UG
A newborn baby has unilateral ptosis with significant levator function deficit (<4 mm). What is the BEST surgical approach?
AFasanella-Servat (Müller's resection)
BFrontalis sling suspension with autogenous fascia lata
CLevator resection alone
DWait until adulthood for surgical planning
Correct Answer: B — Frontalis sling
Poor levator function (<4 mm) = sling surgery required. Fasanella-Servat only works for good LF (≥8 mm). Frontalis muscle acts as substitute elevator via sling suspension. Early surgery prevents deprivation amblyopia.
Q3 UG
A 70-year-old woman with involutional lower lid entropion presents with corneal abrasion. What is the FIRST-LINE surgical treatment?
APosterior lamella graft
BOrbicularis myectomy
CEverting sutures alone
DHorizontal lid shortening (medial canthus plication, lateral lid resection)
Correct Answer: D — Horizontal lid shortening
Involutional entropion = age-related lid laxity + aponeurosis dehiscence. Fix lid laxity with horizontal shortening (canthoplasty, medial advancement). Posterior grafts reserved for cicatricial (scar-induced).
Q4 UG
A child presents with a painless, firm nodule in the upper lid for 3 weeks. Lid eversion reveals a yellowish material. What is the diagnosis and immediate management?
AChalazion; incision and curettage immediately
BChalazion; conservative treatment (warm compress, lid hygiene) for 4–6 weeks, then consider intralesional steroid or I&C if persists
CHordeolum; oral antibiotics
DSebaceous carcinoma; immediate biopsy
Correct Answer: B — Chalazion; conservative first
Most chalazia resolve spontaneously with warm compress and lid hygiene. I&C reserved for persistent cases (after 1–3 months). Hordeolum is painful and acute. In child, low sebaceous cancer risk.
Q5 PG
A 70-year-old woman presents with recurrent upper lid chalazia (third episode in 6 months). Fine-needle aspiration biopsy is performed. What malignancy must be ruled out?
ABasal cell carcinoma
BSebaceous gland carcinoma
CSquamous cell carcinoma
DMelanoma
Correct Answer: B — Sebaceous gland carcinoma
Sebaceous carcinoma is the "great imitator" — presents as recurrent chalazion (especially upper lid, from meibomian/sebaceous glands). Skip areas allow spread. Poor prognosis if missed. Recurrence should raise suspicion for malignancy.
Q6 UG
A 45-year-old man with staphylococcal anterior blepharitis presents with lid margin ulceration and loss of lashes (madarosis). What is the characteristic sign?
AGreasy, scaly debris and red lid margin
BChalazia and meibomian gland dysfunction
CCollarettes (epithelial crusts at lash base) and ulcerated margin
DSubconjunctival hemorrhage
Correct Answer: C — Collarettes and ulcerated margin
Staphylococcal blepharitis: collarettes (epithelial sleeves around lash base) + lid margin ulceration + madarosis (lash loss). Seborrhoeic = greasy scales. Posterior = meibomian disease.
Q7 PG
A patient with Bell's palsy (CN VII palsy) develops severe corneal abrasion due to incomplete lid closure (lagophthalmos). Which is the BEST long-term surgical treatment?
AGold weight implantation in upper lid to assist closure
BLevator disinsertion to weaken upper lid opening
CLower lid tightening with horizontal shortening
DBotulinum toxin injection to levator muscle
Correct Answer: A — Gold weight implant
Gold weight in upper tarsal plate uses gravity to assist eyelid closure, protecting cornea. Levator disinsertion is alternative. Gold weight is preferred for permanent facial nerve paralysis with lagophthalmos.
Q8 UG
A 60-year-old with involutional lower lid ectropion presents with epiphora and conjunctival irritation. What is the underlying pathophysiology?
AOrbicularis hyperactivity
BSevere dry eye
CLid laxity with loss of horizontal tightness and gravity-induced descent
DCN VII paralysis
Correct Answer: C — Lid laxity with gravity descent
Involutional ectropion = age-related ligamentous laxity + gravity. Lacrimal punctum everts, lacrimal drainage impaired → epiphora. Treatment: horizontal lid shortening, medial canthoplasty.
Q9 PG
A 55-year-old presents with a 2-week history of painless lid lump at lower lid medial canthus with recent central ulceration and rolled margin with telangiectasia. What is the most likely diagnosis and treatment?
ASebaceous carcinoma; full-thickness lid resection
BBasal cell carcinoma (rodent ulcer); Mohs micrographic surgery
CSquamous cell carcinoma; wide local excision
DKeratoacanthoma; observation
Correct Answer: B — BCC; Mohs surgery
Classic "rodent ulcer" (central ulceration + rolled margin + telangiectasia) at lower lid medial canthus = BCC (most common malignant lid tumour, 80%). Mohs micrographic surgery = gold standard (highest cure, tissue sparing).
Q10 UG
A patient with acute right-sided Bell's palsy develops exposure keratopathy. Which of the following physical signs would you expect?
Avon Graefe's sign (lid lag on downgaze)
BLagophthalmos (incomplete lid closure) with Bell's phenomenon positive (eye rolls up)
CDalrymple's sign (lid retraction)
DBielschowsky head tilt
Correct Answer: B — Lagophthalmos with positive Bell's phenomenon
CN VII palsy → orbicularis paralysis → lagophthalmos (incomplete closure). Bell's phenomenon (involuntary upward eye movement) is intact, protecting cornea partially. But with significant lagophthalmos, corneal exposure and abrasion occur.

University / MBBS Exams
University Final MBBS
Classify ptosis and describe the management of aponeurotic ptosis with good levator function. Discuss the surgical options available. (5 marks)
Congenital, aponeurotic, myogenic, neurogenic, mechanical. Aponeurotic + good LF: Fasanella-Servat (small ptosis) or levator aponeurosis advancement/resection (larger ptosis). Preserves LF and restores normal lid crease.

Eyelid Anatomy

  • Anterior lamella: Skin + orbicularis (CN VII)
  • Posterior lamella: Tarsus + conjunctiva + LPS (CN III) + Müller's (sympathetic)
  • Meibomian glands: In tarsus; lipid secretion; dysfunction → posterior blepharitis

Ptosis Grading & Treatment

  • LF ≥12 mm: Normal
  • LF 8–12 mm: Fasanella-Servat or levator resection
  • LF 5–7 mm: Fasanella-Servat or levator resection
  • LF <4 mm: Frontalis sling (autofa fascia lata)

Entropion vs Ectropion

  • Entropion (inturned): Involutional = lid laxity; Wies procedure or horizontal tightening
  • Cicatricial: Posterior lamella graft (hard palate)
  • Ectropion (everted): Involutional = laxity; horizontal lid shortening; Cicatricial = skin graft

Lid Lesions

  • Chalazion: Painless, meibomian; warm compress, intralesional steroid, I&C if persistent
  • Hordeolum: Painful, acute (Zeis/Moll or meibomian); antibiotics, drainage
  • BCC: Most common malignant, lower lid medial canthus, rodent ulcer → Mohs surgery
  • Sebaceous carcinoma: "Great imitator," masquerades as chalazion, upper lid, poor prognosis
